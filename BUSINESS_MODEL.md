# Business model and market position

## Customer and problem

**Target buyers:** e-commerce retailers, consumer brands, marketplaces, and commerce agencies with product catalogs and permissioned customer interaction data. Likely users include retention/CRM managers, merchandising teams, and customer experience analysts.

**Problem hypothesis:** broad segments and generic campaigns can miss individual customer intent, repeat irrelevant content, and make product discovery less useful. HyperPersonal demonstrates one workflow for translating profile signals into a ranked product edit, a next action, and a tailored storefront/marketing draft.

**Value proposition:** make personalization decisions inspectable. The demo shows the profile signals, recommendation components, action rationale, uncertainty, and simulated reward trade-offs so operators can review why an experience was selected.

This is a product hypothesis for a hackathon prototype. There are no validated customers, pilots, measured lift, or demonstrated revenue savings.

## SaaS model and monetization hypothesis

The application includes illustrative Starter ($49/month), Growth ($199/month), Professional ($599/month), and custom Enterprise price cards. These amounts are demo placeholders, not approved prices or market research.

Potential monetization paths to validate include:

- Subscription tiers based on catalog size, monthly profile/event volume, and number of business workspaces.
- Enterprise fees for access controls, regional deployment options, data retention, integrations, auditability, and support commitments.
- Optional onboarding or integration services for catalog and CRM adapters.

Pricing should be tested against customer willingness to pay, infrastructure cost, measurable business value, and the operational cost of privacy/compliance controls. No paid tier is currently enforced in the product.

## Bangladesh relevance and international readiness

Bangladesh-based online retailers could benefit from more relevant product discovery and customer retention tools as their digital catalog and customer operations mature. This is a potential use case, not a validated market finding or partnership. Local evaluation should include Bangla and English language quality, local payment/shipping catalog attributes, locally appropriate currency and pricing, consent expectations, and reliable low-bandwidth/mobile use.

The schema includes market and language fields and is designed for future localization. The current catalog, currency, copy, product imagery, and sentiment lexicon are English/US-oriented; international or Bangladesh deployment is not implemented or validated. A production service would require locale-aware currency, translations, regional hosting/data handling choices, and country-specific legal review.

## Competitive distinction to test

The prototype emphasizes explainability and user review alongside personalized outputs. Its differentiation should be evaluated with merchants and compared with existing commerce/CRM personalization tools; no competitive advantage is claimed as established fact.

## Business readiness limits

The system uses synthetic data and simulated outcomes only. There is no live commerce connector, authenticated multi-tenant SaaS, billing, production analytics, service-level commitment, or measured model performance.
